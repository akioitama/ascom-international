# Design references

Reviewed on 9 November 2026 from the public homepages. The notes are about structure and pacing. No layout, colour, sentence, photograph, or logo from these sites is used on ASCOM.

ASCOM’s own logo sets the palette: near-black, a steel blue close to the mark, and grey, on an off-white page. Corners stay square. Motion is limited to the hero and a few section reveals, and it is switched off when the visitor asks for reduced motion.

## Valmont Industries — https://www.valmont.com/

- Homepage: a full-bleed photograph and a short line (“Conserving Resources. Improving Life.”), then a “scroll to explore” cue, a short company paragraph, and numbered routes into About, Infrastructure, and Agriculture.
- Header: corporate, quiet, with the business areas easy to reach.
- Hero: one strong image, very little text, no card grid on top of the photograph.
- Type: large confident headlines, short supporting copy.
- Images: infrastructure photography at a large size, not thumbnails.
- Projects: dated story cards. ASCOM does not invent dates to imitate this, so ASCOM projects are large and image-led without a fake date.
- Animation: the page asks the reader to scroll; movement is secondary to the photograph.
- Footer: headquarters address and phone, plus routes to leadership, news, and investors.
- Adapted for ASCOM: one photographic hero, a short company statement, numbered capability entries, large project rows, and a footer that leads with addresses and phone numbers. Leadership and investor modules are omitted because ASCOM has not published that material.

## Oshkosh Corporation — https://www.oshkoshcorp.com/

- Homepage: a purpose line, then a wide grid of product families (access equipment, fire apparatus, refuse, airport rescue, concrete, and others), each with its own image.
- Header: group-level navigation across many brands.
- Hero and type: short, industrial, people-and-purpose language rather than a feature list.
- Product layout: each family is a large tile, not a specification table on the homepage.
- Technology: themes such as electric vehicles, intelligent products, and digital manufacturing are presented as ideas, separate from the vehicle tiles.
- Footer and careers: a clear invitation into culture and jobs.
- Adapted for ASCOM: vehicle work (fire crash trucks, foam units, water trailers, bodies) is given the same kind of large tile treatment, using ASCOM photographs only. Technology stays a separate, plainly worded division because ASCOM’s page lists equipment categories rather than a technology platform.

## SWARCO — https://www.swarco.com/

- Homepage: a long-standing line (“The Better Way. Every Day.”), a short heritage note, then three expertise pillars and a further breakdown of road-marking types.
- Navigation: organised around solution families a buyer can browse.
- Product layout: categories first, benefits second, with room for technical depth on inner pages.
- Images: product and road context, used to explain a range.
- Footer: group identity and a way to start a requirement.
- Adapted for ASCOM: road-safety products and road signs are a catalogue with categories, filters, and one URL per item. Where SWARCO would show a datasheet, ASCOM shows the specification only if one was published, and otherwise says the detail has not been published yet.

## Rosenbauer — https://www.rosenbauer.com/en

- Homepage: product-first. A vehicle story, a monthly vehicle, and clear routes into apparatus, equipment, and service.
- Product pages, from the public positioning: galleries, equipment families, and a way to enquire.
- Type and images: the vehicle is the hero. Text does not compete with it.
- Careers and heritage sit beside the product story, not instead of it.
- Adapted for ASCOM: the fire crash truck, foam unit, and cylinders have their own product URLs, galleries from the existing project pages, and an inquiry button. No performance figure, pump rating, or chassis specification was added to imitate a Rosenbauer data sheet.

## Hill & Smith Infrastructure — https://hillandsmithinfrastructure.com/

- Homepage: a safety headline, a plain explanation of permanent and temporary vehicle-restraint and hostile-vehicle work, then solution groups and a direct phone and email.
- Projects and knowledge: the public site presents case studies and technical material as things a buyer can open, not as marketing decorations.
- Contact: the number and address are part of the page, not only the footer.
- Adapted for ASCOM: construction and road safety read as solution lists a customer can scan. The resource centre has real categories. Empty categories stay empty, with a clear line that the file has not been uploaded, which is the honest version of a knowledge centre. Phone, email, and both addresses are on the contact page and in the footer.

## Larsen & Toubro — https://www.larsentoubro.com/

- Homepage: full-width editorial headlines, then a split between industries and businesses, a sustainability statement, careers, and a running list of corporate news.
- Header and footer: the footer carries the legal identity, registered office, and contact route. The header can carry a large group without looking like a brochure.
- Projects: scale is communicated with real assignments and dates. ASCOM does not borrow that scale of claim.
- Adapted for ASCOM: the information architecture is a group site (about, businesses, products, projects, industries, resources, careers, contact) rather than a single landing page. Sustainability is limited to the four practices the manufacturing page actually names. There is no investor module and no carbon-neutral claim.

## What the ASCOM site takes from the set

- A quiet header, a sticky bar, and one obvious inquiry action.
- A full-bleed photograph with a short headline.
- Divisions and products as large linked panels.
- Projects as image-led records, with only the facts the company published.
- A catalogue that can grow by admin entry rather than by a code change.
- A footer that is mostly addresses, hours, and real links.
- Motion limited to entrance and hover, with `prefers-reduced-motion` respected.

## What it deliberately does not take

- Competitor colour systems, icons, photography, and sentences.
- Investor, stock, and leadership modules without source material.
- Animated statistics.
- A multi-coloured card grid, glass panels, or decorative blobs.
- Datasheets filled with estimated engineering numbers.
